# Positive Quality Anchor

Use this reference for every Generate, Edit Target, Prompt-only, and finished-frame review. It defines the positive visual target. Passing the anti-filter, anti-anime, anti-low-poly, source-topology, and material tests is necessary but not sufficient: the finished frame must also show an authored improvement in composition, colour organization, viewing path, or focal force.

This anchor is a transferable decision system, not a template. Do not generalize any demonstrated subject, prop, landscape, aspect ratio, or exact palette.

## Target Read

At thumbnail scale, the output should feel like a deliberately art-directed painted frame rather than a cautious tracing of the source or a surface-stylized photograph. It should preserve recognition-critical relationships while making one clearly better composition decision.

The target fingerprint couples six observations:

1. **Topology survives:** identity, count, action, gaze, prop state/contact/orientation, landmark relation, adjacency, depth order, and the dominant motion line remain recognizable when declared.
2. **Distribution is authored:** unless explicitly protected, output ratio, crop, headroom, focal scale, major area shares, negative space, horizon placement, and local contrast distribution may be redesigned to strengthen the first read.
3. **A viewing path exists:** one primary macro departure and one supporting move connect the dominant field, focal event, gesture/prop, and exit or counterform.
4. **Colour has spatial jobs:** broad colour fields, a structural counter, a controlled focal apex, a neutral bridge, and a dark or light anchor create richness through ownership rather than a global saturation increase.
5. **Large marks carry structure:** large visible strokes may describe a cloud volume, field current, water flow, fold fan, wall plane, or another named form; they are not automatically a brush-slab failure.
6. **Materials remain non-interchangeable:** the scene may share one painted medium, but skin, hair, cloth, foliage, sky, metal, water, rock, or architecture must still differ in plane scale, mark direction, edge, and reflectance.

## Protect Topology, Decide Distribution

Record these separately before prompting:

- **Topology anchors:** identity, pose/action, gaze, prop type/state/contact/orientation, left-right order, adjacency, occlusion, depth order, landmark relation, protected geometry/colour, and key motion line.
- **Distribution permissions:** output aspect ratio, crop, headroom, subject/prop scale class, exact versus relational position, horizon placement, foreground share, negative-space share, and colour-area allocation.

Do not infer that topology protection locks distribution. “Keep the person on the right and the tower on the left” normally protects the relationship; it does not require the original pixel coordinates, headroom, or scale unless the user says exact, fixed, unchanged, or pixel-locked. Conversely, do not restage an item whose exact placement, ratio, or documentary geometry the user protected.

For a strong painterly remake with no exact-distribution request, prefer **Balanced or Style-first transformation with Expressive abstraction** over a Restrained tracing. Preserve a short anchor set, then select one distribution change that survives at 128–256 px.

## Positive Optimization Bundles

Choose one primary bundle and at most one supporting bundle. Do not apply every move.

### Focal-scale and crop

- change the output ratio or crop when it creates a stronger frame;
- enlarge a face, body, prop, landform, void, or light shape enough to change its scale class;
- remove passive headroom or peripheral territory while preserving the declared relational zone and motion line.

### Area and negative space

- give one sky, wall, field, water, shadow, architecture, snow, or atmosphere mass more or less territory;
- turn a source-owned quiet region into an active pressure shape or breathing field;
- group repeated scenery into a few rhythm masses instead of preserving documentary distribution.

### Light and colour topology

- reshape the large light/dark boundary or the adjacency of colour zones;
- keep protected local colours but rebalance their area, value, chroma, or surrounding neutral field;
- create a contained dark or light anchor that stabilizes a high-chroma frame.

### Viewing-path reinforcement

- align cloud, field, road, garment, gesture, architecture, shadow, or current directions so they lead through the focal event;
- let the supporting move reinforce the primary departure instead of forming a second competing composition.

## Rich Colour Without Global Saturation

When a richer, more memorable colour result serves the source, use a role-and-area plan:

- **Dominant field:** the broad atmosphere or environment family;
- **Structural counter:** a second large owner that separates depth or subject;
- **Focal apex:** the cleanest chroma, strongest hue boundary, or most concentrated colour event;
- **Neutral bridge:** cloth, cloud, stone, skin, architecture, haze, or another low-chroma owner that gives the saturated fields room;
- **Anchor:** a contained deep dark or bright light that prevents an all-midtone, washed-out frame.

Large saturated fields are allowed. Quieting context should first reduce hue noise, small edge density, microcontrast, and competing texture—not automatically bleach, gray, or desaturate every non-focal region. The result should remain coloured and spatial at thumbnail scale.

Do not copy a fixed palette. The owners may use analogous, complementary, warm-cool, near-monochrome, value-led, or saturation-led relationships according to the source.

## Structural Large-Mark Test

A large mark passes when all are true:

- it belongs to a named material or atmospheric mass;
- its direction and edge follow volume, flow, gravity, perspective, light, or motion;
- several marks interlock into a coherent macro form rather than sit as isolated stamps;
- its scale is subordinate to the mass it describes and does not tile unrelated regions;
- another material would require a visibly different mark grammar.

A large mark fails when it is an opaque decorative rectangle, repeated stamp, or flat slab that replaces volume, depth, and light without a structural job.

## Positive Acceptance Gate

Before calling a result successful, answer yes to every applicable item:

1. Does the result preserve the declared topology anchors?
2. Does it make one better composition decision at thumbnail scale rather than only adding paint texture?
3. If distribution was transformable, did crop, focal scale, area, negative space, light topology, or colour-zone adjacency visibly improve the first read?
4. Is the colour memorable because roles and areas are controlled, not because saturation was raised everywhere?
5. Is there a neutral bridge plus a dark or light anchor when the frame uses large high-chroma fields?
6. Do the largest strokes describe volume, material, or directional flow?
7. Do focus, support, and context retain different edge, mark, and contrast budgets?

A technically safe but visually timid output does not pass a request for strong stylization. A bold output that breaks identity, action, prop state, landmark relation, or another protected anchor also fails.

## Sample-Residue Boundary

Never universalize a positive example's person, umbrella, field, mountain, cloud pattern, pose, exact crop, exact hue trio, or brush stamp. Generalize only the authored relationship: topology preserved; distribution deliberately redesigned; colour assigned by spatial role; large marks tied to form; and focal hierarchy improved at thumbnail scale.
