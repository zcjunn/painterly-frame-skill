# Painterly Continuity Contract

Use this reference for every Generate, Edit Target, Prompt-only, or finished-frame review task. Read `composition-color-lock.md` first: this file explains how to render the locked design as one connected painted field; it does not choose crop, focal scale, exposure key or palette roles. It closes the terms that weaker image models often reduce to flat colour blocks, cut-paper collage, cel shading, posterization, or a global paint filter.

The target is the visual character of the initial Painterly Frame system: a few clear masses, generous quiet space when the scene supports it, one continuous atmosphere, and brushwork that builds light, volume, material and directional flow. The image may be graphic, but it must still feel painted through one connected spatial field.

## Preserve the Initial Aesthetic

Do not treat later cross-model precision as permission to make every frame busier, tighter, harder-edged or more structurally explicit.

- A small subject inside a large sky, field, wall, fog bank, water body or darkness can be the strongest composition.
- Preserve an already effective source ratio, headroom, quiet-area share and subject scale. Change them only when the user allows it and the change clearly improves the first read.
- For a supplied photo, preserve the exact original pixel aspect ratio by default. Measure width and height from the source file, not the resized preview, and do not substitute a stock canvas; if the runtime lacks that exact preset, use the closest supported ratio without stretch or unintended crop and disclose it.
- A macro departure may reshape light, colour adjacency, cloud volume, field current or viewing path inside the existing distribution. It does not have to enlarge the subject or replace the crop.
- Painterly richness comes from connected colour turns, edge variety and directed marks—not from extra objects, uniform texture, more polygons or maximum detail.

## Closed Vocabulary

Use the operational meaning, not the bare label.

| Term | Operational meaning | Common weak-model shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Large colour mass | A continuous scene-owned region with internal light turns, material behaviour and a relationship to neighbouring masses | one unmodulated flat fill or pasted polygon |
| Painterly plane | An unequal colour/value turn caused by form, light, fold, material or atmosphere; its edge may be hard, broken, soft or lost | equal geometric tiles, two-band cel shading or posterized steps |
| Interlocking brush field | Several overlapping strokes that share a direction or volume and collectively build one form | isolated brush stamps, rectangular slabs or texture laid over a finished photo |
| Soft transition | Overlapping low-contrast strokes that turn a form or merge it into atmosphere while retaining colour | blur, airbrush fog or plastic gradient |
| Broken edge | A contour that appears and disappears because strokes cross it, light changes, or atmosphere reduces contrast | dotted outline or random erasure |
| Lost edge | A local boundary where adjacent values and colours merge because subject and environment share light or atmosphere | the entire object becoming blurry |
| Colour echo | A small, motivated reflected or repeated hue that connects two spatial owners under the same light | decorative accent copied everywhere |
| Surface texture | The smallest residue of brush drag, grain or scumble after form already reads | a universal canvas overlay used to simulate painting |

## Construction Order

Build and describe the frame in this order. A later layer cannot rescue a disconnected earlier layer.

1. **Composition field:** choose the ratio, quiet-area share, focal scale and viewing path. Preserve strong source distribution when it already carries the emotion.
2. **Three value groups:** dominant quiet field, supporting structural mass, and small apex. These remain readable without texture.
3. **Five-to-nine scene masses:** name owners, area, overlap and direction. Do not render them as isolated cutouts.
4. **Boundary relationships:** assign how every important adjacent pair connects through overlap, turning form, cast shadow, reflected colour, atmosphere or a deliberate graphic seam.
5. **Internal colour turns:** give each important mass one broad base and a few unequal shifts that describe light, volume or material. Avoid uniform fill.
6. **Interlocking strokes:** build volume or flow with overlapping, directionally related marks. Keep every mark smaller than the form it describes.
7. **Material accents:** add the smallest hard edges and high-frequency marks only where they support the focal path.

## Boundary Relationship Map

For each important adjacency, choose one primary relationship and optionally one supporting relationship:

- **Occlusion:** a short hard edge where one form physically crosses another; lose or soften the edge away from the focal point.
- **Turning form:** two-to-four overlapping colour turns describe curvature or changing orientation; do not close the form with a line.
- **Contact:** a compressed shadow, colour transfer or shared directional stroke binds touching forms to the same ground.
- **Atmospheric merge:** contrast, edge hardness and mark frequency decrease with distance while broad colour and silhouette remain.
- **Reflected-colour bridge:** one owner's light or local colour appears subtly in its neighbour, proving shared illumination.
- **Graphic seam:** a deliberately flat or sharp break used sparingly for the story or focal rhythm; it must be balanced elsewhere by softer or broken connections.

A major subject or object fails continuity when most of its perimeter uses the same hard edge, when it has no contact or reflected-colour relation to the scene, or when every surrounding mass stops exactly at its contour like a sticker.

## Stroke Field

### Stroke hierarchy

- **Structural strokes:** broad, overlapping marks that establish a cloud volume, field current, fold fan, wall plane, water flow, rock weight or light corridor.
- **Turning strokes:** medium marks that change direction or colour as a surface turns.
- **Accent strokes:** sparse small marks at eyes, hands, prop seams, foam crests, grass tips or other story-critical details.

The three scales must coexist. A frame made only from giant slabs feels assembled; a frame made only from small marks feels noisy or filtered.

### Stroke continuity rules

- Let neighbouring strokes overlap, taper, break and partially reveal the colour beneath; do not place every stroke as an isolated opaque stamp.
- Align stroke direction to form or flow. Clouds swell around light; grass leans with wind and depth; cloth radiates from tension; hair groups along weight and motion; water follows current.
- Change mark scale and edge with depth: broader and quieter in atmosphere, more directional in the middle distance, selectively sharper near the focal event.
- Carry at least one directional current across more than one object or region when the scene supports it, such as cloud-to-light, road-to-gesture, field-to-body or shadow-to-doorway. This creates a viewing path rather than separate decorated pieces.
- Do not demand visible brush texture in every region. Quiet negative space may use only a few broad tonal turns and soft scumbles.

## Colour Integration

All materials exist under one motivated light field.

- Preserve local colours, then let the key light, shadow family and reflected light influence them differently according to material.
- Use one or two colour echoes to connect distant masses, not a repeated accent pattern.
- At a hard hue boundary, retain value structure and add either a reflected-colour bridge, contact shadow, broken edge or shared directional stroke.
- Avoid filling each object with one unrelated local colour. Even graphic colour blocks need internal light turns and a shared illumination relation.
- Avoid global LUT language as the main colour solution. Colour ownership is spatial; integration happens at boundaries and inside material turns.

## Material-Owned Brush Behaviour

Select only materials present. These are behaviours, not mandatory words.

| Material | Connected construction | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Skin | quiet overlapping wedges and curved colour turns; sparse hard accents; soft reflected colour from nearby cloth or sky | flat peach fill, porcelain gradient, dark complete contour |
| Hair | one dominant mass with several overlapping ribbons and broken silhouette clumps | helmet shape, equal glossy stripes, strand inventory |
| Cloth | broad planes joined by strokes that radiate from gravity, tension and contact points | unrelated triangles, every fold outlined, same texture as skin |
| Grass/foliage | clustered depth masses linked by a common wind/current; a few foreground directional blades | leaf-by-leaf noise, uniform neon fill, identical upright strokes |
| Sky/cloud | broad atmospheric fields with interlocking soft-edged strokes that turn around light | flat blue background plus pasted white blocks, rectangular slabs |
| Water | overlapping ribbons and broken foam seams aligned to current | equal short strokes everywhere, white texture pasted on blue |
| Rock/earth | weight-bearing interlocking planes with a few broken granular accents near focus | random low-poly mosaic, universal speckle |
| Metal/glass | compact reflected planes and a few crisp seams that echo surrounding colour | full glossy outline, chrome applied to unrelated materials |

## Portable Continuity Packet

Write this before runtime-specific wording:

```text
Painterly continuity:
- Composition field: [quiet-area owner and share; focal scale; preserve or change source distribution and why].
- Boundary map: [important adjacency -> occlusion / turning form / contact / atmospheric merge / reflected-colour bridge / graphic seam].
- Internal turns: [each focal/support mass has a broad base plus a few unequal light/material colour turns; no unmodulated fills].
- Stroke field: [structural current; turning-stroke direction; sparse accents; scale change with depth].
- Shared light: [key-light colour/direction; shadow family; one or two reflected-colour bridges].
- Material divergence: [three present materials and visibly different stroke/edge behaviour].
- Continuity gate: [what prevents cutout, poster-block, airless or global-filter appearance].
```

The final generation prompt can be shorter, but it must express every populated line with visible owners and verbs. Do not copy the template brackets.

## Weak-Model Prompt Compression

Do not paste the entire analysis or every vocabulary definition into a weaker runtime. Long inventories can dilute the scene and make the model satisfy rules as separate visual objects. Compile the contract into six scene-specific clauses, in this order:

1. **Preserve:** one sentence for subject/topology plus the source distribution strength to keep.
2. **Massing:** one sentence naming the three value groups and the five-to-nine masses as spatial owners, not a list of shapes.
3. **Internal turns:** one sentence requiring a broad base plus a few unequal overlapping light/material colour turns in the focal and supporting masses.
4. **Connections:** one sentence naming the two or three most important adjacencies and their physical/colour relationships.
5. **Stroke and material:** one sentence naming one structural current and no more than three important material behaviours.
6. **Failure correction:** one sentence containing only the most likely or already observed behaviour adapter.

Prefer concrete verbs such as `overlap`, `turn`, `merge`, `taper`, `break`, `reflect`, `lean`, `radiate` and `fade with distance`. Avoid stacking vague style adjectives such as `painterly`, `beautiful`, `faceted`, `cinematic`, `rich`, `hand-painted` without visible actions.

For a first attempt on an unknown runtime, use no adapter unless its known behaviour justifies one. For a retry, keep clauses 1–5 unchanged and replace only clause 6 with the matching adapter. This isolates the failure instead of changing the entire art direction.

## Behaviour-Based Adapters

Keep the portable contract unchanged. Add at most one adapter for the observed failure; add a second only when source topology also drifted.

### Cut-paper / colour-block collage

```text
Unify the frame into one painted light field. Replace long uniform object borders with mixed edge ownership: short hard occlusions near focus, overlapping colour turns on curved forms, broken strokes at moving/material edges, and lost segments in shared atmosphere. Every major mass needs internal light variation plus one physical or colour connection to a neighbour; no isolated flat cutouts.
```

### Poster fill / cel-band shortcut

```text
Do not use one flat fill or two hard value bands per object. Build each important mass from one broad base plus a few unequal overlapping colour turns caused by light, form or material, with no complete ink contour and no equal polygon tiling.
```

### Brush-stamp / slab shortcut

```text
Do not place opaque rectangular stamps as clouds, fields, walls or water. Use several tapered, overlapping strokes whose direction and edge follow the named volume or current; keep marks smaller than the mass and let some under-colour remain visible.
```

### Global texture / filter shortcut

```text
Remove universal canvas grain and repeated brush noise. Rebuild silhouette, value groups, boundary relationships and material-owned strokes first; texture may remain only as sparse residue that differs by material and focus.
```

### Sticker subject / airless depth shortcut

```text
Bind the focal subject to the scene with contact shadow, reflected local colour and at least one lost or broken contour segment. Reduce edge hardness and mark frequency with depth while preserving broad coloured atmosphere; do not blur the whole background.
```

## Continuity Tests

1. **Cutout-perimeter test:** trace the focal silhouette and main environment masses. A complete uniform hard perimeter fails.
2. **Neighbour-connection test:** every important mass must name at least one overlap, contact, atmospheric, reflected-colour or shared-current relation to a neighbour.
3. **Flat-fill test:** each important mass must contain visible unequal colour/value turns tied to light, form or material; a single unmodulated fill fails.
4. **Stroke-field test:** structural, turning and accent scales must be distinguishable; isolated equal stamps or one repeated size fail.
5. **Shared-light test:** materials retain local identity while participating in the same key, shadow and reflected-light field.
6. **Depth-frequency test:** edge and mark frequency change across foreground, middle distance and atmosphere without collapsing the background into blur.
7. **Texture-removal test:** removing grain and tiny marks must leave composition, value, depth and light intact.
8. **Quiet-space test:** large negative space remains intentionally simple when it strengthens mood; do not fill it merely to prove painterliness.

Pass only when the frame reads first as one connected painted scene and second as distinct shapes and materials. A collection of individually attractive colour blocks is not enough.
