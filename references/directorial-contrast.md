# Directorial Abstraction and Contrast Ownership

Read this for every Generate, Edit Target, Prompt-only, and finished-frame review. It is the control layer for changes that must remain visible at thumbnail scale.

The objective is not maximal distortion. It is an authored shot whose focal hierarchy, area relationships, color events, and contrast ownership are deliberately different from an unexamined photographic layout.

Read [composition-color-lock.md](composition-color-lock.md) first. The objective is not to force every Edit Target away from a strong source distribution: Preserve-and-enrich may keep the same crop, headroom, focal scale, horizon and quiet-area share while changing light topology, colour adjacency, edge rhythm or viewing current. The macro-departure magnitude contract below is mandatory only for Directed-restage/Style-first/Expressive/Radical work.

## Start with a Dramatic Proposition

Write one sentence describing what the finished frame should make the viewer feel or notice first. Phrase it as a visible relationship, not a mood adjective.

Examples of structure, not presets:

- a small figure is pressed down by a ridge that has become an angular crown;
- two faces nearly collide while the room is reduced to one quiet colored wall;
- a doorway expands into a light void that swallows the surrounding street;
- a hand or tool crosses the frame as a foreground barrier while the body becomes secondary;
- an empty snow field opens around one dark track and a distant warm rupture.

The proposition must name an actor, a pressure/counterforce, and the intended first read. Do not write only “dramatic,” “stylized,” or “more abstract.”

## Build a Directorial Transform Proposal

Choose one **primary macro departure** and one **supporting move**. The primary departure must alter a major area, contour, overlap, scale relationship, viewing path, or light/color zone strongly enough to survive reduction to roughly 128–256 px.

Primary macro departure options:

1. **Scale takeover:** enlarge a face, hand, prop, landform, void, or light source until it changes the composition family.
2. **Area reallocation:** let sky, wall, shadow, water, snow, architecture, crowd, or atmosphere gain/lose substantial visual territory.
3. **Crop/restage:** move from documentary inclusion to an authored close, partial, asymmetric, or compressed staging while preserving declared anchors.
4. **Foreground occlusion:** introduce or enlarge a source-owned foreground mass that crops, frames, divides, or pressures the focal event.
5. **Perspective or depth compression:** steepen convergence, collapse depth bands, stretch one spatial interval, or use controlled foreshortening.
6. **Negative-space takeover:** convert quiet sky, fog, wall, water, or darkness into an active shape that controls tension.
7. **Light-shape rearchitecture:** turn a source light, mist, reflection, shadow, window, or glow into a large wedge, island, seam, corridor, or aperture.
8. **Color-zone restaging:** change the area and adjacency of dominant, structural, focal, and neutral color regions rather than applying a global grade.
9. **Subjective graphic rupture:** use flat paint, collage, scratches, drawn motion, discontinuous perspective, or symbolic fragments only when a psychological/event motive exists.

Supporting moves may come from the same list or from contour simplification, directional rhythm, local hue boundary, atmospheric banding, edge suppression, or repeated-detail grouping.

### Magnitude Contract

For Style-first/Expressive work, the proposal is insufficient when it only:

- recenters the same layout;
- changes crop by a negligible amount;
- adds brush texture, fog, bloom, rim light, particles, grain, or scratches;
- preserves the same horizon, major silhouettes, area ratios, and focal size while changing color;
- writes two or three “exaggeration axes” that remain visually minor;
- makes every object slightly more angular without changing hierarchy.

At least one of the following must be true at thumbnail scale:

- a major mass occupies a clearly different share of the frame;
- the source and result have different dominant silhouettes or overlaps;
- the focal actor changes scale class or its relationship to the environment;
- the negative-space or light-shape topology changes;
- a new large color-zone adjacency becomes part of the viewing path;
- a source-owned element becomes a foreground aperture/barrier or a major counterform.

Do not use a universal percentage. Judge whether the change creates a different composition decision, not merely a different rendering.

## Separate Fidelity from Distribution

Preserve identity, count, action, gaze, protected geometry, required text and recognition-critical colour. For Preserve-and-enrich, also preserve the source distribution recorded in the lock. For Directed-restage, do not automatically preserve:

- exact headroom or empty-space ratio;
- documentary subject scale;
- exact positions of incidental trees, rocks, windows, folds, lights, snow clumps, leaves, branches, or crowd members;
- every depth band or atmospheric interval;
- the source crop and horizon;
- the source's local contrast distribution.

If architecture, product geometry, map structure, text, or documentary topography is protected, keep it stable and direct the macro departure into crop, foreground, light shape, atmosphere, color zoning, or surrounding transformable space.

## Assign Contrast Ownership

Contrast is a budget, not one global slider. Audit and assign these independent dimensions:

- global value span;
- local value contrast;
- microcontrast/midtone definition;
- edge hardness and edge density;
- chroma/saturation;
- hue-boundary strength and hue noise;
- texture frequency/detail density;
- FX/motion density.

Use three saliency tiers:

### Tier 1 — Focal owner

Give the focal actor/event the richest useful combination, usually two or three strong dimensions rather than all eight. Examples: a hard eye edge plus skin/background value separation; a saturated energy seam plus a light-dark rupture; a dark figure plus a warm face inside a pale field.

### Tier 2 — Supporting structure

Keep enough contrast to explain depth, gesture, architecture, or counterforce. Use medium edges and broader value/color steps. It may echo one focal color quietly but must not create a competing apex.

### Tier 3 — Context field

Compress local value range, microcontrast, edge density, hue noise, and texture frequency. Preserve broad silhouette, spatial layering, material family, and motivated color. The context may remain bright, dark, detailed, or colorful, but it should read as a unified mass before its individual objects.

Only the dimensions needed for the focal read should peak. Do not make the subject simultaneously the brightest, darkest, sharpest, most saturated, most textured, largest, and most central unless overwhelming spectacle is the explicit narrative.

## Chromatic Gray, Not Gray Fog

Non-focal regions may recede through chromatic gray: blue-gray, green-gray, violet-gray, warm stone gray, desaturated local color, or another scene-owned near-neutral.

To keep “gray” colorful and spatial:

- retain one dominant hue family and small value shifts across depth;
- preserve local-color traces so snow, wood, skin, stone, foliage, metal, and haze do not become one substance;
- merge hue variations that create noise, but keep changes tied to light, material, or distance;
- use a few broad silhouette or perspective separators instead of uniform blur;
- avoid a full-frame gray veil, lifted black fog, or desaturation overlay.

The background should feel quiet and colored, not dead, muddy, or disconnected from the light.

## Author a Color Collision Only When It Solves the Frame

Color collision means a localized discontinuity that owns attention. It can be:

- hue opposition;
- warm/cool separation;
- saturation rupture inside a muted field;
- bright chromatic light against a dark colored mass;
- near-white or near-black interruption of a colored field;
- local color resisting colored illumination;
- an abrupt material/color boundary;
- a small focal family pressed against a much larger analogous field.

For every collision, declare:

1. the two owners;
2. where they touch or transition;
3. which side dominates in area;
4. what the collision says about attention, depth, relationship, or event;
5. which protected colors must remain stable.

If the source already has a strong, quiet palette, do not invent a collision. Preserve-and-refine or analogous/value-led organization may be more specific. Never treat warm-cool, teal-orange, purple-green, or red-blue as a mandatory signature.

## Shot-scale and Scene Decisions

- **Portrait/character:** consider a face, hand, shoulder, coat, weapon, or foreground body as a scale-takeover shape; keep identity anchors, not photographic headroom.
- **Relationship:** use overlap, unequal scale, opposing profiles, gaze corridors, or an active dark/bright wedge; avoid evenly spaced side-by-side staging.
- **Environment:** give one landform, structure, void, weather mass, road, river, or light shape actor status; preserve the semantic relationship, not incidental distribution.
- **Architecture/product:** protect required geometry; direct abstraction into crop, aperture, light, reflection, atmosphere, surrounding masses, and color zoning.
- **Action:** make one verb readable through pose, foreshortening, diagonal, crop, and environment before FX.
- **Quiet/high-key:** preserve luminosity; create hierarchy with one anchoring dark/chromatic mass, edge selection, and colored-gray context rather than global underexposure.

## Prompt Block

Compile the proposal into visible instructions:

```text
Directorial proposition: [actor] is [visible pressure/relationship] against [counterforce]; the first read is [event].
Primary macro departure: [specific scale/area/crop/occlusion/perspective/negative-space/light/color-zone change] so [major silhouette or area relationship] differs from the source at thumbnail scale; use this field only when the Composition Lock is Directed-restage.
Supporting move: [specific secondary change]. Preserve [declared anchors]. For Preserve-and-enrich, replace these two fields with [light shape/colour adjacency/edge rhythm/viewing current] and keep the locked distribution.
Contrast ownership: Tier 1 [owner] receives [selected value/edge/chroma/texture dimensions]; Tier 2 [support] retains [medium structural cues]; Tier 3 [context] becomes a unified [chromatic-gray or scene-owned colored] field through reduced [local contrast/microcontrast/edge density/hue noise/texture frequency], while preserving [depth/material cues].
Color collision: [none, preserve existing, or explicit owners/adjacency/dominance/function].
Thumbnail target: source and result must differ in [major mass/contour/overlap/focal scale/light topology/color-zone adjacency], not only in surface texture.
```

## Multi-scale Review

Review source and output side by side.

### 128–256 px / blur map

- Does the result have a different major mass, area ratio, contour, overlap, focal scale, light shape, or color-zone relationship?
- Is the dramatic proposition legible without brush texture?
- Does one focal island own the strongest useful contrast dimensions?
- Do non-focal regions merge into one or two colored structural fields rather than dozens of equal details?

### Mid scale

- Does the primary macro departure improve beauty, tension, rhythm, or story rather than feel random?
- Is the viewing path clear from context to support to focal owner?
- Does the color collision, if used, have a spatial boundary and dominant side?

### Close scale

- Are material planes and selective marks supporting the macro design rather than compensating for a literal layout?
- Does context retain colored material/depth information after microcontrast reduction?

Style-first/Expressive fails when source and result remain essentially interchangeable in the thumbnail/blur map even if close-up painterliness is attractive.
