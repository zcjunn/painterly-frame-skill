# Variation Engine

Use this for multiple directions, batches, or when the first concept feels generic.

## Recipe Tuple

Define each variant as:

```text
<composition family, shot scale, dramatic proposition, primary macro departure/supporting move, value plan, focal-support-context contrast ownership, color-authorship mode/primary contrast/color-collision decision, light geometry, motion/pose or environmental verb, abstraction strength, material emphasis, FX grammar>
```

Keep source invariants fixed. For a real new variant, change at least three nontrivial axes, including either composition family or shot scale. Moving the same subject a few pixels or swapping accent hue is not a variant.

For Style-first/Expressive variants, each direction also needs a thumbnail-visible primary macro departure. Variants that preserve the same major area ratios, silhouette overlaps, focal scale, and light/color topology are one rendering recipe, not different directorial concepts.

## Composition Choices

- aperture frame
- environment canyon
- isolated void
- opposed profiles
- diagonal action
- circular engine
- threshold stage
- monument and current
- ruptured strata

## Value Plans

- **Dark reserve:** 65–75% quiet dark, readable midtone subject, 3–8% light apex.
- **Split exposure:** one bright plane against one deep structural mass, focal subject crossing the boundary.
- **High-key anchor:** 55–70% pale field, one dark subject/foreground anchor, small chromatic accent.
- **Near-monochrome rupture:** narrow value/color family with one sharply opposed seam, eye, prop, or core.

## Color-authorship Strategies

Audit the source once, preserve protected anchors across the batch, then vary function rather than merely swapping hues. Use `color-authorship.md`.

- Preserve-and-refine with a value-led narrow hue family;
- Rebalance through analogous harmony plus a saturation hierarchy;
- Rebalance through local hue-boundary separation at equal-value forms;
- Re-script with one dominant complementary or split-complementary family and a quieter counter-family;
- Re-script with warm-cool organization only when light, material, or story benefits;
- near-monochrome field with one focal rupture;
- protected local color under a distinctly colored light;
- muted dominant/structural families with one controlled chromatic apex and neutral bridge.

Every variant must still name dominant, structural, focal, and optional neutral/connector owners. Do not reuse exact published palettes, treat warm-cool as mandatory, or call an accent-hue swap a new direction.

## Shot and Motion Choices

- intimate close-up with lost background;
- compressed two-shot with opposing diagonals;
- medium action with one readable verb;
- wide environment with small human anchor;
- low-angle machine/architecture dominance;
- high-angle vulnerability or entrapment;
- canted instability, used sparingly.

## Material Emphasis

Select one dominant material world and one counter-material:

- worn painted metal vs skin;
- dry stone/plaster vs glass or energy;
- soot/cloth vs polished mechanism;
- wet haze vs dry graphic line;
- organic membrane/foliage vs hard tool/armor.

## Environment Abstraction Axes

For environment-emphasis variants, preserve the same semantic anchors while changing the transformation logic, not merely the season or hue. Choose one primary move and one supporting move per variant:

- scale hierarchy and hero-form prominence;
- contour rhythm;
- perspective compression/expansion;
- overlap or foreground aperture;
- negative-space placement;
- light geometry;
- color zoning;
- atmospheric layer compression;
- directional rhythm of repeated elements.

The primary move should differ between environment variants when abstraction is part of the request, and it must change a major blur-map relationship rather than only surface detail.

## FX Grammars

Use only if the scene needs an effect:

- sharp seam + broad directional ribbons;
- flat technical circle + sparse sparks;
- fractured membrane + particulate core;
- toxic vapor + thin line symbols;
- pressure wave + displaced debris silhouettes;
- red/black tendrils + white rupture.

## Batch Diversity Gate

For each pair of variants, check:

- different composition or shot scale;
- different quiet-area placement;
- different value plan;
- different color function, not just hue;
- when color varies, a different justified authorship mode/primary contrast or role-ownership map—not a global grade;
- different light geometry;
- different primary macro departure or major area/overlap/color-zone relationship;
- different focal/support/context contrast budget when hierarchy is being explored;
- different pose/viewing path;
- for environment-emphasis, different hero/counterform staging or exaggeration-axis combination;
- no repeated sample residue.

If fewer than three axes differ, redesign before generation.
