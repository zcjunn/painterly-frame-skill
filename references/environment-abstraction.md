# Environment Abstraction and Exaggeration

Read this when the environment carries the focal event, no inspectable character/object anchor is present, or the user explicitly permits nonliteral scene reconstruction.

Apply it together with `directorial-contrast.md`. This module chooses environment-specific forms; the directorial module sets the magnitude, contrast ownership, and thumbnail difference contract.

## Default Contract

Treat a landscape, street, interior, industrial space, or architectural view as designed visual drama rather than scenery to be faithfully repainted. Absence of a character must not reduce style strength.

- **Balanced or protected geometry:** use Restrained abstraction unless the user grants more freedom.
- **Style-first environment-emphasis:** default to Expressive abstraction unless the user requests faithful topography, architecture, product geometry, mapping, or documentation.
- **Radical abstraction:** use only when the user explicitly prioritizes invention over structural fidelity.

Exposure remains independent. Expressive abstraction may be high-, mid-, or low-key.

Color authorship also remains independent. Abstracting shapes does not automatically authorize a new palette; audit and choose Preserve-and-refine, Rebalance, or Re-script from `color-authorship.md`.

Expressive success is not counted by the number of named axes. Require one primary macro departure that changes the composition decision at 128–256 px, plus one supporting move. Radical work requires at least two primary-level departures.

## Give the Environment an Actor and a Counterforce

Choose one **hero form**: the landform, structure, void, weather mass, or light shape that must dominate first read. Choose one **counterform/current** that pushes against or leads toward it: river, road, shadow wedge, forest wall, cloud band, machinery line, crowd flow, or another source-owned directional force.

Phrase their relationship as a visual verb: presses, splits, climbs, funnels, erupts, folds, suspends, swallows, opens, or resists. This is compositional behavior, not permission to invent a new story object.

Examples of translation, not fixed recipes:

- a ridge can become an angular crown opposed by a dark ascending forest wedge;
- water can become a broad graphic ribbon that pulls the eye through rock islands;
- a street can become converging slabs cut by one luminous corridor;
- an interior void can expand until surrounding columns act as an aperture;
- cloud, spray, fog, dust, or snow can become a shaped quiet mass instead of uniform atmosphere.

## Preserve Anchors, Not Distribution Maps

Keep the semantic minimum and a short set of recognition anchors: usually the principal landform/structure, foreground-middle-distance relationship, dominant direction, source ratio when requested, and any protected identity or geometry.

Unless declared invariant, do not preserve exact counts or positions of trees, stones, windows, branches, waves, rubble, lights, or snow clumps. Group repeated units into a few rhythm clusters. The source supplies evidence and relationships; it is not a tracing plate.

When recognizability permits, allow controlled shifts in horizon, crop, scale, overlap, spacing, contour, atmospheric depth, local perspective, light shape, and color-zone area. Source fidelity protects the declared relationship; it does not require the same empty-space ratio or scenic distribution. Never distort protected architecture, products, faces, text, maps, or documentary geometry merely to satisfy this module.

## Macro-shape Compression

Before material marks, reduce the frame to roughly five to nine interlocking masses. A useful set is:

1. quiet atmosphere or void;
2. hero form;
3. counterform/current;
4. foreground anchor or aperture;
5. focal light or chromatic rupture;
6. one or two optional transition masses.

Merge most repeated micro-elements into those masses. Keep only a few sharp representatives near the focal path. If the thumbnail still depends on dozens of individually legible trees, rocks, bricks, leaves, windows, or reflections, compression is insufficient.

## Macro Departure and Supporting Axes

Choose one primary departure that strengthens the source's own gesture and changes a major area, contour, overlap, focal scale, negative space, light shape, or color-zone relationship. Add one supporting axis. Do not maximize every axis and do not treat axis count as evidence of visible transformation.

| Axis | Visible decision | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Scale hierarchy | enlarge the hero form or shrink subordinate fields so dominance is unmistakable | uniform scenic scale |
| Contour rhythm | sharpen, stretch, bow, fracture, or simplify the source's dominant silhouette | random deformation unrelated to source |
| Perspective | steepen convergence, compress depth, or widen one spatial interval | implausible camera with no focal gain |
| Overlap/occlusion | let a foreground mass crop or frame the hero form more aggressively | filling every corner with equal detail |
| Negative space | enlarge sky, fog, wall, water, or shadow into an active quiet shape | empty space with no compositional role |
| Light geometry | turn light, mist, shadow, or reflection into a readable wedge, beam, pool, seam, or island | uniform volumetric haze |
| Color zoning | spatially assign large hue/value/chroma roles from the chosen color-authorship plan | global grade or automatic warm-cool split |
| Atmospheric layers | collapse or expand selected depth bands to stage the focal event | evenly softened distance |
| Directional rhythm | align rocks, trunks, roofs, waves, debris, or seams into a controlled cadence | repetitive asset scatter |

### Thumbnail Magnitude Gate

Before material rendering, compare the source and proposed result as five to nine flat masses. Expressive/Radical fails when the same mountain/forest/river, street/building/sky, room/wall/floor, or coast/sea/cloud areas remain in nearly the same proportions and positions while only their textures change.

At least one of these must be legible without small detail:

- the hero form takes over or relinquishes clearly more space;
- the counterform crosses, presses, frames, or divides the hero in a new way;
- an active void, foreground occluder, or light shape becomes a major mass;
- perspective/depth intervals change the visual path;
- a new large color-zone adjacency reorganizes attention;
- the composition family changes through crop, overlap, scale, or silhouette.

Minor recentering, gentle mountain enlargement, slightly denser brushwork, more fog, a stronger vignette, or a global palette shift are no-ops for this gate.

## Beauty and Coherence

Exaggeration must improve hierarchy, rhythm, and tension, not merely make forms strange.

- Balance one dense or jagged region against one quiet, broad field.
- Use asymmetry with a counterweight rather than random imbalance.
- Repeat one directional motif two or three times at different scales, then interrupt it near the focal event.
- Let the viewing path move from foreground cue to hero form to light/color apex; avoid multiple equal destinations.
- Keep the most descriptive edges and color breaks near the focal route. Let the rest remain incomplete.
- Compress non-focal tree, rock, window, roof, wave, snow, foliage, or rubble fields into one or two scene-owned chromatic-gray/colored masses. Lower local contrast, microcontrast, edge density, hue noise, and texture frequency while retaining broad depth bands and material cues.

## Material Translation After Shape Design

Apply material-specific marks only after the macro design works:

- snow, sand, plaster, and cloud: broad quiet planes with a few broken boundaries;
- mountain, cliff, rock, and masonry: interlocking facets with selective structural seams;
- forest, grass, crowds, and repeated verticals: grouped wedges or columns plus a few sharp representatives;
- river, road, rails, smoke, and wind: long directional bands or ribbons;
- glass, ice, wet ground, and metal: limited planar reflections rather than continuous gloss.

Add a restrained flat graphic layer—broken contours, dry-brush vectors, simplified shadow seams, or hard color boundaries—to interrupt optical realism. Do not use magic particles as a substitute for abstraction.

## Prompt Requirements

Do not rely on words such as “stylized,” “painterly,” “abstract,” or “dramatic.” State visible departures from the source:

- what remains invariant;
- the hero form and counterform/current;
- which repeated details are merged;
- the selected abstraction strength;
- the primary macro departure, its major area/contour/overlap impact, and one supporting move;
- the thumbnail difference target and which incidental distributions are not preserved;
- focal/support/context contrast ownership, including how non-focal regions are quieted without a gray fog overlay;
- the macro value/light topology and justified color-authorship mode, primary contrast, role ownership, and protected anchors;
- distinct mark behavior for the important materials.

Reject conventional scenic realism, literal asset counts, photographic underpainting, generic matte-painting polish, all-over brush texture, and atmosphere that merely softens the source without rebuilding it.
