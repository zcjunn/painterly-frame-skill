# Research Basis and Boundaries

Read this only for methodology audits, rationale, or reconciliation with new reference sets.

## Evidence Design

The visual system was derived from a broad comparison of animated-film colour keys, matte paintings, background paintings, layouts, and finished frames rather than a small set of publicity images. The sample covered:

- multiple productions, exposure keys, and scene categories;
- bright and dark exposure keys;
- ordered civic space, layered industrial space, organic/transcendent space, and abstract FX space;
- close-up, two-shot, medium action, wide environment, and quiet transition;
- human acting, relationship, architecture, action, magic/technology FX, and atmospheric closure;
- macro thumbnail hierarchy, meso composition/material staging, and micro face/edge/texture treatment.

The set intentionally mixed color scripts, environment paintings, character studies, and finished frames. Counts are approximate because some portfolio sheets combine subpanels.

The sample was intentionally reviewed as small thumbnails and grouped contact sheets so that brush texture and micro-detail could not hide weak macro hierarchy. It was used to inspect area allocation, silhouette/overlap, focal scale, colour-zone adjacency, exposure diversity, and focal-versus-context contrast ownership. No copyrighted frame is bundled in the skill.

Only traits recurring across distinct scene categories were promoted to the fixed system. Exact palettes, characters, props, locations, runes, and layouts remain variables or sample residue.

## Production and Design Sources

The generalized observations are consistent with established animation-layout, background-painting, colour-script, cinematography, and digital-painting practice: depth and dynamic camera can coexist with 2D imperfection; story-first colour and light precede surface detail; and environment, matte, effects, and texture each need distinct responsibilities.

## Color-method Integration

Operational color decisions use standard design vocabulary—hue/value/chroma, analogous and complementary relationships, warm-cool organization, saturation contrast, dominant/subordinate/accent roles, local color under illumination, adjacency, neutral balance, and simultaneous separation. These are candidate methods, not claims that the studio follows one disclosed formula.

Colorist/cinematography integration also draws on primary technical sources:

- [Blackmagic Design — DaVinci Resolve Color](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve/color): separate control of contrast, hue, saturation, tonal zones, midtone detail, secondary regions, and thumbnail/lightbox comparison.
- [American Cinematographer — Key Light vs. Background Illumination](https://staging.ascmag.com/articles/key-light-vs-background-illumination): preserve the intended tonal/texture rendition of the principal face while balancing background illumination independently.
- [ARRI — DP Kim Ji-yong on colored shadows and narrative color contrast](https://www.arri.com/news-en/dp-kim-ji-yong-netflix-squid-game-2-alexa-35-arri-lighting): colored shadow depth and opposing color fields are shaped around narrative tension and spatial transition, not used as a universal palette.

Select a method only after auditing the source and story. Cross-check the proposed role structure against the researched color keys, but never sample an exact published palette or promote one sequence's hues into a universal preset.

## Stable Conclusions

1. The style is not a fixed dark-purple palette. It is story-specific color scripting inside a stable contrast topology.
2. Three large value groups and one focal event survive more consistently than any exact hue.
3. Central prominence is perceptual and often asymmetrical; small subjects can dominate through isolation.
4. Light sources have readable geometry and semantic purpose.
5. Painterly identity comes from faceted planes, selective brush boundaries, material-specific marks, and edge hierarchy—not one filter.
6. Spatial depth and graphic 2D effects coexist deliberately.
7. Quiet fields, foreground apertures, haze, and occlusion are active compositional tools.
8. Action is pose/perspective first and particles second.
9. Color scripting is functional and scene-specific: exact hues vary while dominant field, structural counter, and controlled focal ownership remain comparatively stable.
10. Warm-cool opposition is one variable solution, not a fixed identifier; value, chroma, analogous harmony, near-monochrome rupture, and local-color/illumination contrast also recur as valid organization tools.
11. Strong color treatment can preserve, rebalance, or re-script a source palette. The common requirement is deliberate spatial/material ownership rather than maximum color difference.
12. Strong style recognition begins in shot design: crop, focal scale, overlap, negative space, light shape, and color-zone area are established before painterly surface treatment.
13. In Style-first/Expressive remakes, at least one primary macro departure must remain visible at 128–256 px; multiple named axes with negligible magnitude are not equivalent.
14. Non-focal regions often recede through a combined reduction of local contrast, microcontrast, hard-edge density, hue noise, and texture frequency while remaining scene-owned colored fields with broad depth and material cues.
15. Background “gray” is commonly chromatic rather than neutral: blue-gray, green-gray, violet-gray, warm near-neutral, or desaturated local color preserve atmosphere without equal competition.
16. Color collision is a local, owned event. It may be hue, temperature, value, saturation, material, or area rupture; it is optional and should have owners, adjacency, a dominant side, and a narrative/attention function.
17. Fidelity protects identity and declared geometry; it does not automatically protect photographic headroom, incidental distribution, source crop, horizon, or local contrast allocation.

## Copyright and Provenance Boundary

No researched still, color-script image, background painting, contact sheet, or episode screenshot is bundled as a skill asset. The skill stores generalized observations only. The temporary study remains outside the skill and is not reusable content. The skill explicitly excludes character identity, published text, logos, signature motifs, exact props, locations, camera layouts, and color-script sequences from reusable prompts.

When new references are added, analyze them as evidence. Do not copy them into generated output or silently replace the fixed system based on one sample.
